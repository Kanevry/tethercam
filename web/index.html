Two apps · one cable · open source

Your iPhone, instead of a webcam

Two apps, one cable. TetherCam for Mac is a free menu bar app, TetherCam for iPhone is free on the App Store: download both, plug the USB cable in, done. Your phone becomes the system camera “TetherCam” and you pick it like any webcam in Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, FaceTime, QuickTime Player, Safari or Chrome — no Wi-Fi, no cloud, no pairing code. Streaming or recording? The OBS plugin is the pro path and adds its own TetherCam source to OBS Studio, with the phone’s audio on the same cable.

TetherCam for Mac → Free .dmg, v0.3.0, signed and notarized TetherCam for iPhone → Free on the App Store, iOS 17 or later

Works with: Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, FaceTime, QuickTime Player, Photo Booth, Safari, Chrome — and any other Mac app that lists system cameras, Webex, Slack or Discord among them — through the TetherCam Mac app. OBS Studio gets a source of its own, with the phone’s audio, through the plugin; run one receiver at a time. Verified in Zoom 7.1.5 and Microsoft Teams on 2026-09-10. Read the quick start, about five minutes.

App 2 of 2 · the Mac app

The second of the two apps

TetherCam for Mac is a small menu bar app with its own camera extension. It publishes the iPhone as the system camera “TetherCam”, 1920x1080 at 30 fps, so Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, FaceTime, QuickTime Player, Photo Booth, Safari, Chrome and every other Mac app that lists system cameras see it like any webcam. No OBS running, no Wi-Fi, still just the cable.

TetherCam-mac.dmg → v0.3.0, signed and notarized Checksum → TetherCam-mac.dmg.sha256

Free and open source (MIT), macOS 14 or newer, Apple silicon and Intel. Not on the Mac App Store. Prefer a cask? brew tap kanevry/tethercam then brew install --cask tethercam.

Five steps

  1. Install TetherCam on the iPhone from the App Store and open it.
  2. Plug the iPhone into the Mac with the USB cable. The first time, tap Trust on the phone.
  3. Download the .dmg, drag TetherCam into Applications with Finder, then open it. It lives in the menu bar and has no window.
  4. macOS asks once to allow the camera extension: System Settings, General, Login Items & Extensions, Camera Extensions, switch TetherCam on. That takes your administrator password once, and the menu bar app has a button that opens exactly that pane.
  5. In any app, pick the camera TetherCam. That is it; it reconnects by itself when the phone app restarts.

Where to pick the camera

  • Zoom: the camera menu next to the video button.
  • Google Meet: the arrow next to the camera button.
  • FaceTime: the Video menu.
  • QuickTime Player: New Movie Recording, then the arrow next to the record button.
  • Photo Booth: the Camera menu.
  • Safari and Chrome: the camera picker of the site you are on.
  • Microsoft Teams: Settings, then Devices.
  • Anything else that lists system cameras, Webex, Slack or Discord among them: its own camera or video settings.

Verified on 2026-09-09 with a real iPhone 15 Pro Max in QuickTime Player, Photo Booth, FaceTime, Google Meet in Chrome, Safari, Chrome and ffmpeg, and on 2026-09-10 in Zoom 7.1.5 and Microsoft Teams. Webex, Slack and Discord were not tested here; they read the same system camera list, so the camera appears there like any webcam, but we have not verified them ourselves.

What it does not do

  • Video only. A macOS camera extension cannot carry audio. For sound in a call use the phone’s microphone via Continuity or the Mac microphone; for audio and video together use the OBS plugin.
  • One receiver at a time. The phone serves one connection: run either the Mac app or the plugin source in OBS, not both. The second one says BUSY.
  • Fixed format. 1920x1080 at 30 fps. A phone held upright is pillarboxed into that frame.

If something is off

The menu says “Error: TetherCam.app must be in /Applications”, but it is

Something other than Finder copied the app while it still carried the quarantine flag, so macOS App Translocation runs it from a random read-only path. Drag the app into /Applications with Finder, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/TetherCam.app and open it again.

No camera called “TetherCam” shows up

The camera extension is not enabled yet: System Settings, General, Login Items & Extensions, Camera Extensions, switch TetherCam on. Apps read the camera list when they launch, so quit the app completely (Cmd-Q) and reopen it. Still missing? Restart the Mac: macOS finishes registering a newly installed or updated camera extension only at the next boot. Zoom and Teams listed TetherCam right after that restart.

The picture stays black, or the menu says BUSY

Another receiver already holds the phone. Close the TetherCam source in OBS, or whatever else is connected, then reconnect. And make sure TetherCam is open and in the foreground on the phone; iOS suspends the listener as soon as the app goes to the background.

In OBS: Tools, then add the camera

Choose Tools, then TetherCam: Add iPhone camera to current scene. That creates the source and fits it to your canvas. Plug the phone in, unlock it, open TetherCam and leave it in the foreground.

If the picture stays black

The first row of the source properties is a status line that names the missing piece: no phone attached, app not in the foreground, or the live format once frames arrive. The app has a matching diagnostics sheet for the phone side.

The data path

Not magic, three boxes and a cable

The iPhone captures with AVCaptureSession and encodes HEVC in hardware, then serves the stream on a local TCP port, 7878. The Mac reaches that port through usbmuxd, the same system service that carries Xcode traffic over the cable. The OBS plugin decodes with VideoToolbox and hands the frames to the canvas.

  • 1080p30 by default, 1080p60 possible.
  • About 13 Mbit/s HEVC, encoded in hardware on the phone.
  • Ping round trip over USB about 1 ms.
  • First frame about 90 ms after the source connects.
  • Reconnect after a cable pull in about 1.3 s.
  • No frame buffering in the plugin, each decoded frame goes straight to the canvas.
  • Auto horizon leveling with CoreMotion and the rotation coordinator.
  • Camera picker in the app: Back Wide, Back Ultra Wide, Back Telephoto, Front.

Rotation belongs on the capture side. The app levels against the horizon and rebuilds the encoder session when the geometry changes, so the Mac receives a picture that is already right way up.

The two apps, no OBS: install TetherCam for iPhone and the TetherCam Mac app (a free, signed and notarized .dmg), enable its camera extension once, and pick the camera “TetherCam” in Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, FaceTime, QuickTime Player, Photo Booth, Safari or Chrome. FAQ

Questions worth asking first

Can I use my iPhone as a webcam in Zoom, Teams, Meet or FaceTime on a Mac?

Yes, with two free apps: TetherCam for iPhone from the App Store and the TetherCam Mac app. Install both, plug the USB cable in, enable the camera extension once, and pick the camera “TetherCam” in Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, FaceTime, QuickTime Player, Photo Booth, Safari or Chrome. Nothing else has to be running, and the phone app stays open in the foreground. Verified with a real iPhone 15 Pro Max in QuickTime Player, Photo Booth, FaceTime, Google Meet in Chrome, Safari, Chrome and ffmpeg (2026-09-09) and in Zoom 7.1.5 and Microsoft Teams (2026-09-10). Webex, Slack, Discord and anything else that lists system cameras see it the same way, untested by us. Video only on this path: for the phone’s audio use the OBS plugin.

Does it work without OBS?

Yes. OBS is one of many apps, not a requirement. The two apps are enough: TetherCam for iPhone and the TetherCam menu bar app for the Mac, which carries its own macOS camera extension and publishes the iPhone as a system camera named “TetherCam”, 1920x1080 at 30 fps, with no OBS running. Download TetherCam-mac.dmg (free, MIT, Developer ID signed and notarized, macOS 14 or newer) or run brew install --cask tethercam after brew tap kanevry/tethercam, drag it into Applications with Finder, open it, and allow the camera extension once under System Settings, General, Login Items & Extensions, Camera Extensions. It reconnects by itself after the phone app restarts. It carries video only, and the phone serves one receiver at a time, so run either the Mac app or the plugin source. The Mac app section has the full five steps.

Continuity Camera is not working. Is this an alternative?

Yes, and that is why it exists. Continuity Camera broke with iOS 26.6 against macOS 26.5.2: the handshake succeeded, the picture stayed black. TetherCam is a Continuity Camera alternative that takes a different path entirely, over the USB cable and usbmuxd, so none of the Continuity handshake, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi machinery has to work for it.

Why USB and not Wi-Fi?

The cable is the shortest path. TetherCam needs no Wi-Fi at all: there is no network to configure, no dropouts on a busy conference Wi-Fi, no pairing code, and the video never leaves the machine. The phone also charges while it streams.

How much latency is there?

The ping round trip over USB is about 1 ms, and the first frame arrives about 90 ms after the source connects. The plugin does not buffer frames, it hands each decoded frame straight to the canvas. Measured on one setup, an iPhone 15 Pro Max with an Apple Silicon Mac.

Does it need administrator rights?

Not for the OBS plugin: it installs into your own home folder, with no daemon and nothing system-wide. The TetherCam Mac app is different, because a macOS camera extension has to be approved: you enter your administrator password once, in System Settings, when you enable it.

Which iPhones, which macOS, which OBS?

An iPhone with iOS 17 or later, macOS 12 or later, and OBS Studio 30 or later. Tested on an iPhone 15 Pro Max with iOS 26.6, an Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 26, and OBS 32.2.2.

Does the audio come along?

Yes, since 0.2.0. The app sends the microphone as AAC on the same cable and the source appears in the OBS audio mixer like any other. A mute switch in the app silences it. Needs plugin 0.2.0 on the Mac. A separate microphone in the mixer still works as before.

How do I use an iPhone as a webcam for OBS over USB?

Install the TetherCam plugin for OBS on the Mac, install the TetherCam app on the iPhone, then connect the two with a data cable. In OBS choose Tools, then “TetherCam: Add iPhone camera to current scene”. Unlock the phone and leave the app in the foreground; it shows “Streaming 1080p30” and the picture appears on the canvas. No Wi-Fi, no pairing code, no account and no cloud service are involved, because the HEVC stream travels only through the USB cable.

How does it compare to Camo, EpocCam or Iriun?

Those apps also turn a phone into a webcam, they support Wi-Fi as well as USB, they run on Android and Windows too, and they are closed source with a free tier and a paid version. TetherCam does one thing instead: iPhone to Mac over the cable only, free with no paid tier and no account, and open source, so the wire protocol and both ends can be read and rebuilt. The comparison table lays the rows out side by side.

What does it cost?

Nothing. The iPhone app is free on the App Store, the plugin and the Mac app are free downloads, there is no paid tier, no subscription, no in-app purchase and no account. If it saved you a cable hunt you can buy me a coffee, which is entirely optional.

Is it free and open source?

Yes. The OBS plugin is GPL-2.0-or-later because it links against libobs. The iPhone app, the shared C code, the tools and the protocol spec are MIT. The app collects no data at all, which the privacy policy spells out.

Does it work on Windows or Linux?

No, macOS only for now. The shared C core already builds on Linux, so a receiver for other systems is possible, but none ships today.
