Quick start

Two apps, about five minutes

Download both apps, plug the cable in, done: TetherCam for iPhone captures, TetherCam for Mac publishes the picture as the system camera “TetherCam”. Streaming in OBS Studio instead? There the plugin takes the Mac app’s place. Run one receiver at a time.

A. The two apps: every Mac app

  1. Install TetherCam on the iPhone from the App Store and open it.
  2. Plug the iPhone into the Mac with a data cable. The first time, tap Trust on the phone.
  3. Download TetherCam-mac.dmg and drag TetherCam into Applications with Finder, then open it. It lives in the menu bar and has no window. Homebrew instead: brew tap kanevry/tethercam, then brew install --cask tethercam.
  4. Allow the camera extension once: System Settings, General, Login Items & Extensions, Camera Extensions, switch TetherCam on. Administrator password once; the menu bar app has a button that opens that pane.
  5. In Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, FaceTime, QuickTime Player, Safari, Chrome or any other app that lists system cameras, pick the camera TetherCam. Keep the phone app in the foreground. Details and the picker paths: the Mac app setup guide.

macOS 14 or newer. Video only; choose a separate microphone in your video-call app. For phone audio in OBS recordings and streams, take path B.

If the picture stays black

The first row of the source properties is a status line that names the missing piece: no phone attached, app not in the foreground, or the live format once frames arrive. The app has a matching diagnostics sheet for the phone side.

The data path

What travels through the cable

The iPhone captures with AVCaptureSession and encodes HEVC in hardware, then serves the stream on a local TCP port, 7878. The Mac reaches that port through usbmuxd, the same system service that carries Xcode traffic over the cable. The OBS plugin decodes with VideoToolbox and hands the frames to the canvas.

  • 1080p30 by default, 1080p60 in OBS.
  • About 13 Mbit/s HEVC, encoded in hardware on the phone.
  • Ping round trip over USB about 1 ms.
  • First frame about 90 ms after the source connects.
  • Reconnect after a cable pull in about 1.3 s.
  • No frame buffering in the plugin, each decoded frame goes straight to the canvas.
  • Auto horizon leveling with CoreMotion and the rotation coordinator.
  • Camera picker in the app: Back Wide, Back Ultra Wide, Back Telephoto, Front.

Rotation belongs on the capture side. The app levels against the horizon and rebuilds the encoder session when the geometry changes, so the Mac receives a picture that is already right way up.

Get the OBS plugin

For streaming and recording: the plugin adds its own TetherCam source to OBS Studio, with the phone’s microphone on the same cable and no virtual-camera detour. It installs into your own home folder, so it needs no administrator rights. Restart OBS once after installing. Only calling? The Mac app is the shorter way.

Requirements

  • macOS 12 or later. Tested on Apple Silicon with macOS 26.
  • OBS Studio 30 or later. Tested with 32.2.2.
  • iOS 17 or later. Tested on an iPhone 15 Pro Max with iOS 26.6.
  • A data cable, not a charge-only one.

FAQ

Questions worth asking first

Can I use my iPhone as a webcam in Zoom, Teams, Meet or FaceTime on a Mac?

Yes. Install TetherCam on the iPhone and Mac, connect them with a USB data cable, enable the camera extension once, then choose TetherCam in your call app. This needs macOS 14+ and iOS 17+. Keep the phone app open. Your call uses a separately selected microphone. Start here.

Does it work without OBS?

Yes. The Mac app publishes your iPhone as a system camera at 1080p30. OBS is only needed if you choose the separate OBS plugin. Use one receiver at a time; a second receiver reports BUSY.

Continuity Camera is not working. Is this an alternative?

Yes. I built TetherCam after Continuity Camera connected but left the picture black on my setup. TetherCam takes a separate USB path and does not use the Continuity handshake, Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. The devices do not need a shared Apple Account.

Why USB and not Wi-Fi?

The video goes directly from the iPhone to your Mac through the cable. There is no wireless network to configure, and the phone charges while it streams. Continuity Camera also supports USB; TetherCam uses its own connection instead of Continuity.

How much latency is there?

On one setup, an iPhone 15 Pro Max with an M4 Pro Mac and OBS 32.2.2, USB ping round trip was about 1 ms, and the first frame arrived about 90 ms after connection. These are transport and startup measurements, not end-to-end camera latency. End-to-end latency has not been measured here.

Does it need administrator rights?

Not for the OBS plugin: it installs into your own home folder, with no daemon and nothing system-wide. The TetherCam Mac app is different, because a macOS camera extension has to be approved: you enter your administrator password once, in System Settings, when you enable it.

Which iPhones, which macOS, which OBS?

Both paths need an iPhone with iOS 17+ and a USB data cable. The Mac app needs macOS 14+. The OBS plugin needs macOS 12+ and OBS Studio 30+. Keep the iPhone unlocked with TetherCam in the foreground.

Does the audio come along?

The OBS plugin receives the iPhone microphone in the OBS mixer for recordings and streams, with iPhone app 0.2.0 or later. The Mac app and OBS Virtual Camera carry video only. Choose a separate microphone in Zoom, Teams or your other call app.

How do I use an iPhone as a webcam for OBS over USB?

Install the TetherCam plugin for OBS on the Mac, install the TetherCam app on the iPhone, then connect the two with a data cable. In OBS choose Tools, then “TetherCam: Add iPhone camera to current scene”. Unlock the phone and leave the app in the foreground; it shows “Streaming 1080p30” and the picture appears on the canvas. No Wi-Fi, no pairing code, no account and no cloud service are involved, because the HEVC stream travels only through the USB cable.

How does it compare to Camo, EpocCam or Iriun?

TetherCam is a free, open-source USB option for iPhone and Mac, with no account or paid tier. Other tools offer different platform support and wireless options. The dated comparison table summarizes those differences; check the vendor before choosing.

What does it cost?

Nothing. The iPhone app is free on the App Store, the plugin and the Mac app are free downloads, there is no paid tier, no subscription, no in-app purchase and no account.

Is it free and open source?

Yes. The OBS plugin is GPL-2.0-or-later. The iPhone app, Mac app, shared code, tools and protocol are MIT. There is no account, paid tier or tracking. From version 0.4.0 the Mac app asks GitHub once a day whether a newer version exists; you can turn that off in the menu under “Check for updates automatically”. See the privacy policy.

Does it work on Windows or Linux?

No, macOS only for now. The shared C core already builds on Linux, so a receiver for other systems is possible, but none ships today.
