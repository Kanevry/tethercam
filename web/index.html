Your iPhone as an OBS camera, over the cable

TetherCam sends the iPhone camera into OBS Studio on macOS through the USB cable, with no Wi-Fi, no cloud and no pairing code.

Quick start

Three steps, about five minutes

One package on the Mac, one app on the phone, one menu entry in OBS.

01

Install the OBS plugin

Open the .pkg from the download section or run the one-line installer, then restart OBS. Nothing is installed system-wide and no administrator rights are needed. See the Install guide if anything goes sideways.

03

In OBS: Tools, then add the camera

Choose Tools, then TetherCam: Add iPhone camera to current scene. That creates the source and fits it to your canvas. Plug the phone in, unlock it, open TetherCam and leave it in the foreground.

If the picture stays black

The first row of the source properties is a status line that names the missing piece: no phone attached, app not in the foreground, or the live format once frames arrive. The app has a matching diagnostics sheet for the phone side.

The data path

Not magic, three boxes and a cable

The iPhone captures with AVCaptureSession and encodes HEVC in hardware, then serves the stream on a local TCP port, 7878. The Mac reaches that port through usbmuxd, the same system service that carries Xcode traffic over the cable. The OBS plugin decodes with VideoToolbox and hands the frames to OBS.

  • 1080p30 by default, 1080p60 possible.
  • About 13 Mbit/s HEVC, encoded in hardware on the phone.
  • Ping round trip over USB about 1 ms.
  • First frame about 90 ms after the source connects.
  • Reconnect after a cable pull in about 1.3 s.
  • No frame buffering in the plugin, each decoded frame goes straight to OBS.
  • Auto horizon leveling with CoreMotion and the rotation coordinator.
  • Camera picker in the app: Back Wide, Back Ultra Wide, Back Telephoto, Front.

Rotation belongs on the capture side. The app levels against the horizon and rebuilds the encoder session when the geometry changes, so OBS receives a picture that is already right way up.

Get the OBS plugin

Until the first release is published, build from source (5 minutes). The two links above point at the final release URLs and will start working the moment v0.1.0 is tagged. Full install guide and troubleshooting.

The one-line installer, for the same reason, also waits for that first release:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Kanevry/tethercam/main/scripts/install.sh | bash

Read the script before you pipe it into a shell. It downloads the release bundle and unpacks it into the OBS plugins directory in your home folder, nothing else.

Requirements

  • macOS 12 or later. Tested on Apple Silicon with macOS 26.
  • OBS Studio 30 or later. Tested with 32.2.2.
  • iOS 17 or later. Tested on an iPhone 15 Pro Max with iOS 26.6.
  • A data cable, not a charge-only one.

Video only for now. Use a separate microphone in OBS.

FAQ

Questions worth asking first

How do I use an iPhone as a webcam for OBS over USB?

Install the TetherCam plugin for OBS on the Mac, install the TetherCam app on the iPhone, then connect the two with a data cable. In OBS choose Tools, then “TetherCam: Add iPhone camera to current scene”. Unlock the phone and leave the app in the foreground; it shows “Streaming 1080p30” and the picture appears on the OBS canvas. No Wi-Fi, no pairing code, no account and no cloud service are involved, because the HEVC stream travels only through the USB cable.

Continuity Camera is not working. Is this an alternative?

Yes, and that is why it exists. Continuity Camera broke with iOS 26.6 against macOS 26.5.2: the handshake succeeded, the picture stayed black. TetherCam is a Continuity Camera alternative that takes a different path entirely, over the USB cable and usbmuxd, so none of the Continuity handshake, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi machinery has to work for it.

Why USB and not Wi-Fi?

The cable is the shortest path. TetherCam needs no Wi-Fi at all: there is no network to configure, no dropouts on a busy conference Wi-Fi, no pairing code, and the video never leaves the machine. The phone also charges while it streams.

How much latency is there?

The ping round trip over USB is about 1 ms, and the first frame arrives about 90 ms after the source connects. The plugin does not buffer frames, it hands each decoded frame straight to OBS. Measured on one setup, an iPhone 15 Pro Max with an Apple Silicon Mac.

Does it need administrator rights?

No. The plugin installs into your own home folder, under the OBS plugins directory. There is no system extension and no daemon.

Which iPhones, which macOS, which OBS?

An iPhone with iOS 17 or later, macOS 12 or later, and OBS Studio 30 or later. Tested on an iPhone 15 Pro Max with iOS 26.6, an Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 26, and OBS 32.2.2.

Does the audio come along?

No. Video only for now. Use a separate microphone in OBS.

Is it free and open source?

Yes. The OBS plugin is GPL-2.0-or-later because it links against libobs. The iPhone app, the shared C code, the tools and the protocol spec are MIT. The app collects no data at all, which the privacy policy spells out.

Does it work on Windows or Linux?

No, macOS only for now. The shared C core already builds on Linux, so a receiver for other systems is possible, but none ships today.

Where is the full install guide?

The install guide covers requirements, the Mac plugin, the iPhone app, connecting the two and what to do if the picture stays black, in more detail than the quick start above.
